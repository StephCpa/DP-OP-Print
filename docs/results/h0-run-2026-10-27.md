## 2. Reading

**(a) Three of the four primary AUCs are below 0.5.**

- Approximate two-sided p-values (SE taken from the CI widths, ≈ 0.019):
  - G: p ≈ 0.09 in both groups;
  - control G-Max: p ≈ 0.03;
  - main G-Max: p ≈ 0.6.
- After Holm correction over these four, none is significant. The control G-Max is still not "close to 0.5": it is a marginal reverse-direction result, and v1.3 requires it to be reported as such.

**There are two explanations, and they predict different things.**

1. **Split difficulty imbalance.**
   - With one random 500/500 split, any score's AUC has an SD of ≈ 0.018 around 0.5 even with no membership effect.
   - G is dominated by sample difficulty, so a split whose members happen to be "harder" under θ0 pushes raw G and G-Max below 0.5 *in both groups*. That fits the identical G = 0.468, since both groups appear to use the same split [to confirm].
   - It also fits the θ0-calibrated scores pointing the other way (difference 0.532 / 0.524). Calibration removes difficulty, and what remains is a weak OP-Print-direction effect.
2. **Anti-teacher training (R3; toy R5 lesson 5).** At clip 0.05, members' high-Δ sites are pushed away from the teacher, which raises members' Δ.

**The deciding test** is the θ0-only placebo: the AUC of Δ̄_θ0 for the member labels, on the same rollouts. Explanation 1 predicts it is < 0.5; explanation 2 predicts ≈ 0.5. Training the split's **complement** model cancels explanation 1 by design, and is the cheapest strong check.

**(b) The template spike masks G-Max.** This matches the R1 audit (§2.2) and toy lesson 3.

- In the main group, Δ₁ ≈ 23 nats sits in chunk 1 and wins 76% of argmax chunks. So G-Max mostly reads t = 1, which depends only on the prompts.
- That explains why the main group's G-Max (0.490) is closer to 0.5 than the control's (0.458) while G is identical.
- In other words, the spike adds noise to G-Max rather than signal.

**(c) Δ₁ is not trainable at clip 0.05.**

- The `<think>` entry's summand (≈ 23 nats) is clamped, so its logit only receives a downward push (M1).
- The main group's higher f_rm (0.40 vs 0.09) must therefore come from positions t ≥ 2: the thinking-mode style is learned generically.
- This is the "f_rm met by generic learning" case (toy lesson 8). It is not evidence of leakage, and f_rm must not be used to select the fallback learning rate without the no-r correction (v1.3 §1.1).

**(d) Is Δ₁ membership-free?** Not necessarily.

- Δ₁ depends only on the prompts, but training pushes the clamped `<think>` logit down for members' prompts. If that does not fully generalise, members get a *larger* Δ₁: a reverse-direction signal located exactly at t = 1.
- `gmax_rescore.py` reports Δ₁ for members vs non-members.

## 3. Pre-registered predictions for the two checks (and three cheap extras)

### Check 1: genuine template effect or residual padding bug

`experiments/gpu_checks/first_token_check.py`, run once per group.

**Expected if genuine:**

- **P1.1** At t = 1 the main-group teacher's top-1 token is `<think>` with p ≥ 0.99 in ≥ 90% of samples. Δ₁ / (−log q_S(`<think>`)) lies in [0.95, 1.05], and the top entry carries ≥ 95% of the KL. A 23-nat value means the student's log q(`<think>`) ≈ −23.
- **P1.2** Batched left padding with 0 / 13 / 128 extra pads reproduces the batch-size-1 reference Δ₁ within 0.05 nats (bf16 trunk, fp32 head).
- **P1.3** The pipeline's stored Δ₁ matches the reference within 0.05 nats + 1%, and is constant across the K rollouts of a sample (SD ≤ 0.05).
- **P1.4** In the control group the teacher's top-1 is not `<think>`, and the reference Δ₁ ≈ 1.2 nats.

Any failure of P1.1–P1.3 means a residual bug. The likeliest is P1.3: a padding or batch-composition dependence.

**Check from existing outputs, no GPU.** P1.3's "constant across rollouts" part can be checked now: `gmax_rescore.py` prints the within-sample SD of Δ₁ across the K rollouts.

### Check 2: G-Max without the opening segment

`experiments/gpu_checks/gmax_rescore.py main.npz control.npz`.

- **P2.1** Main-group G-Max from segment 2 lands within ±0.03 of the control's G-Max (0.458), i.e. below 0.5, **not** in 0.59–0.61. If it is ≥ 0.55, the spike was hiding an OP-Print-direction signal that the aligned-template model lacks, and (b)/(c) need revising.
- **P2.2** Control-group G-Max barely moves without segment 1: within ±0.02 of 0.458.
- **P2.3** Dropping only the first 1, 4 or 16 tokens gives the same main-group G-Max as dropping the whole first segment, to within ±0.02. The spike is at t = 1, not spread over the formatting header.

### Extras computed by the same script

- **P3 (placebo).** The θ0-only AUC on this split is < 0.5 (≈ 0.46–0.48). That would mean the sub-0.5 raw AUCs reflect split difficulty imbalance. If it is ≈ 0.50, they are a training effect.
- **P4 (f_rm by position).** The main − control f_rm gap persists without t = 1 and without the first segment (thinking-style learning throughout the rollout).
- **P5 (argmax-chunk share and truncation).** The chunk-1 argmax share is about the same for members and non-members. A clear difference would itself be a membership signature (the synthetic self-test shows how one arises). Truncation rates in vs out differ by less than 3 points.

## 4. How to run

**Self-test.** `python3 experiments/gpu_checks/selftest.py` runs offline in about 30 s and checks both scripts on a tiny random Qwen3 and on synthetic data.

**Check 1.**

- Input: a JSONL with the exact rendered prompts your scorer tokenizes: `{"id", "student_prompt", "teacher_prompt"}`.
- Optional: the stored Δ₁ per rollout as JSON `{id: [Δ₁ for each rollout]}`.
- Command: `python3 first_token_check.py --model <Qwen3-1.7B> --adapter <adapter dir> --prompts p.jsonl --pipeline d1.json --n 32`

**Check 2.**

- Input: one `.npz` per group with `delta [N, K, L]` (NaN after the end of each rollout) and `member [N]`.
- For P3 and P4, also include `delta0` and `dtheta` on the same rollouts.
- Command: `python3 gmax_rescore.py main.npz control.npz`

## 5. After the checks: the next run (v1.3 §5)

H0 failed, so a single model per group is not enough to tell "no leakage" from "leakage hidden by split imbalance". In order:

1. **The complement model of the same split, for each group.** Cheapest, and it cancels the imbalance.
2. **The clip-off arm.** In the toy it was the only clip setting with OP-Print-level leakage (H3's clip-0 arm, needed anyway).
3. **The pre-registered LR fallback,** selected by f_rm^r rather than raw f_rm.
