## 2. Reading

**(a) Three of the four primary AUCs are below 0.5.**

- Approximate two-sided p-values (SE taken from the CI widths, ≈ 0.019):
  - G: p ≈ 0.09 in both groups;
  - control G-Max: p ≈ 0.03;
  - main G-Max: p ≈ 0.6.
- After Holm correction over these four, none is significant. The control G-Max is still not "close to 0.5": it is a marginal reverse-direction result, and v1.3 requires it to be reported as such.

**There are two explanations, and they predict different things.**

1. **Split difficulty imbalance.**
   - With one random 500/500 split, any score's AUC has an SD of ≈ 0.018 around 0.5 even with no membership effect.
   - G is dominated by sample difficulty, so a split whose members happen to be "harder" under θ0 pushes raw G and G-Max below 0.5 *in both groups*. That fits the identical G = 0.468, since both groups appear to use the same split [to confirm].
   - It also fits the θ0-calibrated scores pointing the other way (difference 0.532 / 0.524). Calibration removes difficulty, and what remains is a weak OP-Print-direction effect.
2. **Anti-teacher training (R3; toy R5 lesson 5).** At clip 0.05, members' high-Δ sites are pushed away from the teacher, which raises members' Δ.

**The deciding test** is the θ0-only placebo: the AUC of Δ̄_θ0 for the member labels, on the same rollouts. Explanation 1 predicts it is < 0.5; explanation 2 predicts ≈ 0.5. Training the split's **complement** model cancels explanation 1 by design, and is the cheapest strong check.

**(b) The template spike masks G-Max.** This matches the R1 audit (§2.2) and toy lesson 3.

- In the main group, Δ₁ ≈ 23 nats sits in chunk 1 and wins 76% of argmax chunks. So G-Max mostly reads t = 1, which depends only on the prompts.
- That explains why the main group's G-Max (0.490) is closer to 0.5 than the control's (0.458) while G is identical.
- In other words, the spike adds noise to G-Max rather than signal.

**(c) Δ₁ is not trainable at clip 0.05.**

- The `<think>` entry's summand (≈ 23 nats) is clamped, so its logit only receives a downward push (M1).
- The main group's higher f_rm (0.40 vs 0.09) must therefore come from positions t ≥ 2: the thinking-mode style is learned generically.
- This is the "f_rm met by generic learning" case (toy lesson 8). It is not evidence of leakage, and f_rm must not be used to select the fallback learning rate without the no-r correction (v1.3 §1.1).

**(d) Is Δ₁ membership-free?** Not necessarily.

- Δ₁ depends only on the prompts, but training pushes the clamped `<think>` logit down for members' prompts. If that does not fully generalise, members get a *larger* Δ₁: a reverse-direction signal located exactly at t = 1.
- `gmax_rescore.py` reports Δ₁ for members vs non-members.

## 3. Pre-registered predictions for the two checks (and three cheap extras)

### Check 1: genuine template effect or residual padding bug

`experiments/gpu_checks/first_token_check.py`, run once per group.

**Expected if genuine:**

- **P1.1** At t = 1 the main-group teacher's top-1 token is `<think>` with p ≥ 0.99 in ≥ 90% of samples. Δ₁ / (−log q_S(`<think>`)) lies in [0.95, 1.05], and the top entry carries ≥ 95% of the KL. A 23-nat value means the student's log q(`<think>`) ≈ −23.
- **P1.2** Batched left padding with 0 / 13 / 128 extra pads reproduces the batch-size-1 reference Δ₁ within 0.05 nats (bf16 trunk, fp32 head).
- **P1.3** The pipeline's stored Δ₁ matches the reference within 0.05 nats + 1%, and is constant across the K rollouts of a sample (SD ≤ 0.05).
- **P1.4** In the control group the teacher's top-1 is not `<think>`, and the reference Δ₁ ≈ 1.2 nats.

Any failure of P1.1–P1.3 means a residual bug. The likeliest is P1.3: a padding or batch-composition dependence.

**Check from existing outputs, no GPU.** P1.3's "constant across rollouts" part can be checked now: `gmax_rescore.py` prints the within-sample SD of Δ₁ across the K rollouts.

### Check 2: G-Max without the opening segment

`experiments/gpu_checks/gmax_rescore.py main.npz control.npz`.

- **P2.1** Main-group G-Max from segment 2 lands within ±0.03 of the control's G-Max (0.458), i.e. below 0.5, **not** in 0.59–0.61. If it is ≥ 0.55, the spike was hiding an OP-Print-direction signal that the aligned-template model lacks, and (b)/(c) need revising.
- **P2.2** Control-group G-Max barely moves without segment 1: within ±0.02 of 0.458.
- **P2.3** Dropping only the first 1, 4 or 16 tokens gives the same main-group G-Max as dropping the whole first segment, to within ±0.02. The spike is at t = 1, not spread over the formatting header.

### Extras computed by the same script

- **P3 (placebo).** The θ0-only AUC on this split is < 0.5 (≈ 0.46–0.48). That would mean the sub-0.5 raw AUCs reflect split difficulty imbalance. If it is ≈ 0.50, they are a training effect.
- **P4 (f_rm by position).** The main − control f_rm gap persists without t = 1 and without the first segment (thinking-style learning throughout the rollout).
- **P5 (argmax-chunk share and truncation).** The chunk-1 argmax share is about the same for members and non-members. A clear difference would itself be a membership signature (the synthetic self-test shows how one arises). Truncation rates in vs out differ by less than 3 points.

## 4. How to run

**Self-test.** `python3 experiments/gpu_checks/selftest.py` runs offline in about 30 s and checks both scripts on a tiny random Qwen3 and on synthetic data.

**Check 1.**

- Input: a JSONL with the exact rendered prompts your scorer tokenizes: `{"id", "student_prompt", "teacher_prompt"}`.
- Optional: the stored Δ₁ per rollout as JSON `{id: [Δ₁ for each rollout]}`.
- Command: `python3 first_token_check.py --model <Qwen3-1.7B> --adapter <adapter dir> --prompts p.jsonl --pipeline d1.json --n 32`

**Check 2.**

- Input: one `.npz` per group with `delta [N, K, L]` (NaN after the end of each rollout) and `member [N]`.
- For P3 and P4, also include `delta0` and `dtheta` on the same rollouts.
- Command: `python3 gmax_rescore.py main.npz control.npz`

## 5. After the checks: the next run (v1.3 §5)

H0 failed, so a single model per group is not enough to tell "no leakage" from "leakage hidden by split imbalance". In order:

1. **The complement model of the same split, for each group.** Cheapest, and it cancels the imbalance.
2. **The clip-off arm.** In the toy it was the only clip setting with OP-Print-level leakage (H3's clip-0 arm, needed anyway).
3. **The pre-registered LR fallback,** selected by f_rm^r rather than raw f_rm.

## 7. Follow-up analysis on the exported data (2026-10-27, analysis side)

**Source.** Everything below is recomputed from `data/h0/2026-10-27/`.

**Scripts and outputs.**

- `experiments/gpu_checks/h0_matched_auc.py`, with output in `data/h0/2026-10-27/h0_matched_auc.{json,out.txt}`.
- The first-token numbers come from the `delta1` fields of the prompts JSONL.
- AUC convention as above: lower = member.

### 7.1 The weak base-calibrated signal is split imbalance, not leakage

On split 0A the members are harder under θ0 (placebo AUC 0.454–0.481). Among non-members, the training log-ratio log(S_θ/S_θ0) falls with θ0 level: slopes −0.04 to −0.11 for G, G-Max and G (nc1) in both arms. In other words, generic training shrinks high-Δ samples more. Together these make the "θ − θ0" difference favour members with no memorisation.

Matching members and non-members on θ0 level removes the effect:

| Score (difference θ − θ0) | Main: raw | Main: θ0-decile-stratified | Main: residual on θ0 | Control: raw | Control: stratified | Control: residual |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| G, all tokens | 0.536 [0.499, 0.573] | 0.524 [0.480, 0.555] | 0.503 | 0.529 | 0.493 | 0.482 |
| G, from segment 2 | 0.537 [0.499, 0.570] | 0.498 [0.460, 0.535] | 0.495 | 0.532 | 0.506 | 0.489 |
| G-Max, from segment 2 ("delta-version nc1") | **0.556 [0.522, 0.591]** | **0.502 [0.473, 0.550]** | 0.529 [0.491, 0.568] | 0.539 | 0.504 | 0.514 |

**How the matching works.**

- **Stratified:** AUC within θ0 deciles, weighted by n_in·n_out.
- **Residual:** a quadratic in log S_θ0, fitted on non-members only and cross-fitted, so no non-member scores itself.
- **Validity:** with random labels, the stratified estimator averages 0.499 (SD 0.018).

**The scale-free log-ratio** (θ/θ0) is closer to 0.5 than the difference even before matching (0.505–0.523).

**One exception.** "Main, G-Max on all tokens, stratified" gives 0.538 [0.501, 0.575]. It is driven by the t = 1 spike, which is the argmax chunk in 95% of rollouts, and it is 1 of about 20 tests.

**Reading.** In both arms, no score shows membership information once θ0 level is matched. This confirms the operator's "shared confounding" reading and pins down the mechanism. It also corrects my §2(a): the calibrated 0.52–0.53 is **not** a weak OP-Print-direction effect.

**Design consequence.** Per-model calibrated AUCs must be reported θ0-matched (amendment A1 in design v1.3). Complement pairs remain the primary remedy, because they cancel the imbalance by construction.

### 7.2 What training did, by position

**f_rm (the pre-registered estimator).** The pre-registered definition is a per-member ratio: f_rm = 1 − mean_x D_θ(x)/D_θ0(x). It gives 0.371 (main) and 0.038 (control). The operator's ratio of means (0.397 / 0.086) is a secondary estimator.

| f_rm on members | all tokens | without t = 1 | without segment 1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main | 0.371 | 0.347 | 0.315 |
| Control | 0.038 | 0.041 | **−0.009** |

- **P4 holds.** The main − control gap persists across the whole rollout. The main arm learned the thinking-mode style generically.
- **The control arm barely moved toward the teacher** at lr 5e-6 × 48 steps. Beyond the first segment its f_rm is ≈ 0. A null H0 there is expected regardless of the padding issue.

### 7.3 The first-token KL shifted by 20 nats, on the student side, against the direct gradient

| Δ₁, T = 1.0, mean of k < 8 | θ0 | θ | D_θ (θ0 teacher vs θ student) | θ − θ0 | AUC of the change |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Main | 44.94 | 25.18 | 25.18 | −19.76 (SD 2.23) | 0.520 |
| Control | 2.84 | 1.29 | 2.88 | −1.55 | 0.495 |

**Main arm.** The whole shift is on the student side: D_θ equals θ to 1e-3. The student's log q(`<think>`) at the real t = 1 rose by ≈ 20 nats, uniformly for members and non-members.

That entry is clamped at clip 0.05 (summand ≈ 45 ≫ 0.05), so the direct gradient can only push it **down** (M1). The rise must therefore be indirect. There are two candidates:

1. **The right-padding bug.** In training, t = 1 was read at a pad position for all but the longest prompt in each micro-batch. That trains the model's response to "prompt end + pad", which can spill over to the real t = 1.
2. **Spillover from the generic thinking-style learning.**

**Control arm.** The smaller change is on the teacher side (θ applied to the teacher context). D_θ stays at θ0's value.

The change correlates with teacher-prompt length (r = +0.30), which is consistent with either candidate.

### 7.4 Training-side right padding: what it changes

- **Fidelity.** This is OPSD's upstream default: the collator forces `padding_side="right"` (R1 audit §2.4). OP-Print, if trained with the official code, very likely had the same issue **[inference]**. The existing H0 is therefore the *upstream-faithful* replication, and it did not replicate. The left-padded reruns test *clean* OPSD. Both labels should be kept.
- **Size at clip 0.05.** The bug touches one position per rollout, and its spurious summands (8–25 nats) are clamped. Most of the measured training effect lies beyond t = 1 (f_rm 0.315 without segment 1), so the bug is unlikely to be the main driver of the null. It may still explain the 20-nat Δ₁ shift (§7.3).
- **Clip-off arm.** Without the clip, the spurious t = 1 KL is trained in full. It equals the summed KL of roughly 40–250 ordinary tokens (per-token θ0 KL ≈ 0.1–0.2 nats), and would act on every misread sample at every step. **The clip-off arm must not run with right padding.**
- **Preflight for the fixed trainer** (cheap, before any new training):
  1. Take one training micro-batch at step 0 with `prompt_padding_side=left`, for both the teacher and the student.
  2. Compare the t = 1 logits the trainer slices with the batch-size-1 reference from `first_token_check.last_logits(..., "single")`.
  3. Require a max |ΔKL₁| ≤ 0.1 nats.
  4. Also confirm that the slice index lands on the last real prompt token for every row.

### 7.5 Predictions for the left-padded reruns (recorded before they run)

- **P6 (padding bug vs spillover).** In the main arm at clip 0.05 with left padding, if the right-padding bug caused §7.3, then Δ₁(θ) − Δ₁(θ0) will be > −5 nats (it could even be positive, per M1). If style spillover caused it, the ≈ −20-nat shift recurs (< −15). This needs only the scorer's existing Δ₁ output.
- **P7 (clean H0 at lr 5e-6, clip 0.05).** θ0-matched calibrated AUCs lie within [0.47, 0.53] in both arms (no detectable leakage). The control's f_rm beyond segment 1 stays below 0.1.
- **P8 (clip-off, left padding).** No numeric prediction. The direction comes from the toy (R5): matched AUC higher than at clip 0.05, and the student learns the first token (Δ₁ falls toward 0 in the main arm).

### 7.6 Revised order of the next runs

1. Fix `prompt_padding_side=left` and pass the preflight (§7.4).
2. Run the complement models of split 0A (cancels the imbalance), main and control, with left padding.
3. Run the clip-off arm, with left padding only.
4. **LR fallback.**

   - In the main arm, f_rm is generic style learning, so it can only be used after the no-r correction (f_rm^r).
